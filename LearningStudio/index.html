01 / THE STUDIO
For curious learners · Grade 5 and aboveReal tools. Thoughtful guidance. Room to explore.

An invitation to think with your hands

Some questions deserve
more than an answer.

They deserve a sketch. A measurement. A first attempt that doesn’t quite work—and the chance to try again.

RĀSA Learning Studio brings engineering, visual thinking, and hands-on making into one warm, working space. Explore light, build a mechanism, give a circuit a purpose, or turn a drawing into something you can hold.

01 / The space

An atelier for
ideas in motion.

Warm timber, a flexible central table, and working tools around the edges.

A room that adapts. Movable tables, chairs, and whiteboard make room for discussion, assembly, demonstrations, and robotics testing. Equipment benches remain at the perimeter.

Comforts built in. A separate entrance and walkway, kitchenette with sink and snack refrigerator, full bathroom, heating and cooling, and a timber loft above one end.

From curiosity to something tangible

Look closer.
Find out what happens.

02 / Design, fabrication & print

Give an idea
a physical form.

A drawing becomes a model. A model becomes a test. The next version gets better.

From a flat pattern to a fitted assembly.

Explore structure, material, scale, and presentation through cutting, printing, folding, and iterative prototyping.

Cut, engrave & assemble

  • xTool S1: 40W diode laser and 2W infrared module, with air filter and substantial material supplies.
  • Cricut Maker 4: digital cutting and drawing; additional processes depend on installed accessories.
  • A4 pen plotter: machine-drawn graphics, geometry, and creative coding outputs.

Model, print & present

  • Bambu Lab A1 Mini + AMS: 3D prototyping with automated filament handling and a substantial materials collection.
  • Xerox VersaLink C8000: professional A3 color printing, up to 1200 × 2400 dpi, with oversize media support.
  • HP scanner: bring sketches, studies, and printed work into a digital workflow.

Manufacturer images · Representative configurations. Specifications and image sources.

A possible investigation

Design a small structure, print its plans, fabricate the parts, measure the fit, and revise the joints.

03 / Electronics, robotics & measurement

Make it respond.
Understand why.

A sensor notices. A circuit changes. A measurement helps explain what happened.

A bench for evidence,
as well as invention.

Build circuits and physical-computing experiments, inspect the behavior, then make decisions from what you observe.

Build & program

  • Electronics corner: soldering station and soldering supplies.
  • Arduino boards and accessories: small modules and supporting parts.
  • Components: resistors, LEDs, and assorted electronic supplies.

Measure & inspect

  • Two oscilloscopes: one analog and one digital, with probes and accessories.
  • Digital multimeters and infrared temperature sensors.
  • Professional digital calipers: Mitutoyo and other makes.
  • Digital microscope for close inspection.

Test in physical space

  • Robotics development: combine the studio’s computing, cameras, and electronics in guided prototypes.
  • 300+ large Everbrick blocks: outdoor-stored resources for building and reconfigurable robot obstacle courses.
  • Laser distance meters and a multi-plane laser level for spatial measurement and alignment.
A possible investigation

Build a sensor-driven prototype, inspect its signals, then test its behavior in a reconfigurable obstacle course.

04 / Digital photography & optical sensing

Learn to see.
Then ask how.

Light, lenses, image formation, and the way machines interpret the world.

A photographer’s eye.
An engineer’s questions.

Connect visual storytelling with experiments in lighting, perspective, optics, and computer vision. Photographic resources can be brought in for particular sessions.

Capture & sense

  • USB computer-vision cameras with a selection of interchangeable lens attachments.
  • Digital microscope for material textures and small structures.
  • Photographic cameras and lenses drawn from Mano’s separate Photography Gear Catalog.
  • Mac-based computing for image capture, analysis, and visual experiments.

Shape the image

  • Camera resources: Canon professional DSLRs, an infrared-converted DSLR, and a Leica rangefinder.
  • Lens resources: fisheye, wide-angle, normal, macro, and telephoto optics.
  • Lighting resources: Profoto studio lighting, Canon Speedlites, triggers, umbrellas, and modifiers.
  • Support: tripods and photographic mounting accessories.

05 / Computing, presentation & creative sound

Make the thinking
visible. And audible.

Code, draw, demonstrate, perform, and share what you have learned.

A shared table.
Many ways to explain.

The studio can shift from individual investigation to a group demonstration, design critique, or an experiment connecting code, rhythm, and sound.

Compute & create

  • Several Mac minis, iMacs, and MacBooks, with computing accessories.
  • Local computers support coding, design, equipment connections, and live camera experiments.
  • Remote compute: a possible future resource for demanding projects, rather than an installed studio service.

Present & discuss

  • Sony cinema projector and roll-up projection screen.
  • Sony HD television with Apple TV.
  • Whiteboard for sketches, explanations, and collaborative problem-solving.
  • Movable 8-foot conference table and chairs, plus a speaker system.

Explore music & sound

  • Arturia KeyLab 49-key controller with color display.
  • Alesis Nitro electronic drum kit: red drums with an upgraded sound module.
  • Resources for rhythm, sound experiments, and connections between performance, electronics, and code.
A possible investigation

Turn a sensor reading into a visual or musical response, then explain the system through a live demonstration.
