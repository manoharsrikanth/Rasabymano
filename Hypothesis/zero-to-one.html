Notes on seeing / Origination

The cost
of zero to one.

Working from a question before there is agreement that it can be answered.

The first objection.

When I proposed a camera behind an active display, the objection was reasonable: the display imposes a severe optical penalty. The architectural challenge was to show how the physical structure and computation could work together.

For the first year, I carried the concept largely alone. Later, a small number of people believed in the direction and helped move it forward.

A useful architecture gives an impossible-sounding idea a path into engineering.

Return to first principles.

I repeatedly returned to the fundamentals I had studied at MIT: diffraction, deconvolution and the behavior of optical systems. The point was not to hide the difficulty, but to understand it well enough to shape it.

The eventual product is the visible end of the story. The earlier work is the act of originating the premise and establishing the system that makes it buildable.
