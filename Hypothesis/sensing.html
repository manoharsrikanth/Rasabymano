When the screen
begins to sense.

World-facing cameras, user-facing sensors, and the architecture of a display that understands both sides.

The relationship is the system

The world.
The wearer.
One experience.

A head-mounted display has to understand the world it presents and the person it presents it to. Sensor placement, visual pipelines and the display experience are inseparable architectural decisions.

A practice in sensing.

My Apple work includes the early Vision Products Group and camera architectures associated with visual experience. The public record includes display systems with world and user sensors and a head-mountable-display filing.

The broader patent trail moves toward sensors integrated into the display, camera-less eye tracking, and near-eye systems with photodiodes. The disappearing camera is a useful starting point for understanding that direction.

Software-defined sensing.

The larger question is how much capability can be created through the relationship between a sensing structure and computation. A display can become more than an output surface; it can participate in the sensing itself.
