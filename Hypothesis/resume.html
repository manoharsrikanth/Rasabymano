Executive profile

Manohar Srikanth combines original invention, imaging systems architecture and photographic judgment. His defining contribution is the disappearing-camera concept and its foundational architecture, developed from zero to one before the handoff into product engineering.

His work connects optics, sensors, computation and human experience. The wider record includes sensing displays, head-mounted systems, multi-camera depth, light-field imaging and aerial robotic lighting.

Selected experience

Apple / System and experience architecture

Cameras, sensing and perception systems; work associated with the Vision Products Group and advanced imaging architectures.

Apple / Advanced camera architecture

The disappearing-camera concept and architecture, computational camera prototypes and display-integrated sensing.

Meta / Camera and imaging systems

Camera architecture and system integration for AR and VR devices.

Nokia / Computational imaging

Multi-camera depth architectures and research combining stereo and defocus.

Pelican Imaging / Computational depth

Depth regularization, interactive matting and imaging prototypes.

Autonomous imaging and entrepreneurship

Imaging innovation for autonomous systems and early-stage work combining AI and optics.

Education and research

  • MIT / PhD, 2013. Robotics and computational photography.
  • Indian Institute of Science / MS in computer science, with work in graphics and visualization.
  • Undergraduate electrical engineering / Signal processing, Mysore.
  • Postdoctoral research / Computational cameras.

Creative work and teaching

Photographic exhibitions in Boston; work for MIT publications; coverage of the Dalai Lama’s 2012 MIT visit. Taught a non-credit Digital Photography course at MIT and mentored students and interns.

Computational rim illumination with aerial robots connects the photographic and engineering practices in published research.
