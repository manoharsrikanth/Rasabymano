For curious minds / Learn through seeing

How can a camera
hide behind a screen?

A way into the invention for curious people, young and grown up. Start with a familiar object and ask a different question.

A screen is made
of tiny lights.

A phone screen is made of pixels. A camera needs light from the world to reach its sensor. Put the camera behind the screen and those jobs get in each other’s way.

One way to imagine the idea is a beaded curtain. Light can pass through the spaces, but it arrives changed. The engineering has to shape those spaces and account for what they do to the light.

The display, camera and restoration pipeline are designed together.

Conceptual illustration. Not to scale; the waveform is illustrative, not measured image data.

The hard part is the image.

Light passing through the display can spread and distort. Computation can help restore the image when the system understands how the light was changed. It is not a promise that every blur can simply be reversed; the optics and computation have to be designed together.

Try a way of thinking

Three small
experiments in attention.

These are starting points for curiosity, not product demonstrations.

01 / Observe

Find the opening.

Look at a phone screen. Which parts belong to the picture, and which parts belong to the cameras and sensors?

02 / Change a premise

Move the camera.

Imagine putting the camera behind the screen. What has to change in the screen? What happens to the light?

03 / Connect disciplines

Think as a system.

Which part can the optics solve? Which part can computation help with? What would a person notice?
