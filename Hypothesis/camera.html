The outcome

The screen is whole.

An uninterrupted display and a camera for the person on the other end of a call. The experience is simple; the architecture underneath it is not.

The original contribution

A different question.

In the summer of 2015, I began with a question: could a camera sit entirely behind an active display, rather than forcing a permanent opening into it?

I originated the concept and drove the foundational architecture from zero to one. The system required the display, optics, sensor and computational restoration to be conceived together, before it could be handed into engineering for product realization.

Make the display and the camera occupy the same space. Design the computation around the light that remains.

When I first proposed the architecture, I was told it was impossible. An active display imposes a severe optical penalty. Returning to first principles in optics was how I worked through that objection.

My MIT optics notes were a recurring reference: diffraction, deconvolution and lens behavior. The challenge was to establish an architecture in which the distortion could be understood and controlled.

Optics × Computation

One system.
Three interdependent ideas.

The display must admit light where the sensor needs to see. The camera must capture light that carries the display’s optical effects. Restoration must be designed around those effects.

The display, camera and restoration pipeline are designed together.

Conceptual illustration. Not to scale; the waveform is illustrative, not measured image data.

The architecture goes beyond an optical stack with a software filter added afterward. The display structure and restoration pipeline are part of one design problem. The purpose is an image that serves human perception, not only a recovered signal.

Precision matters

Two products.
Different disappearing acts.

On the iPhone Duo’s inner display, the color FaceTime camera is hidden beneath the screen. On the iPhone 18 Pro Max, the under-display component described by iFixit is the Face ID infrared camera; the selfie camera and dot projector remain in the opening.

The teardown documents the display’s engineered sensor region, including a lower-density pixel pattern and an apparent infrared-pass layer. It provides a public view of the physical implementation.
